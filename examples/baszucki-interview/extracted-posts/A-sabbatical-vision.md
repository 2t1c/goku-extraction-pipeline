# Example post — A. Baszucki's 2-year sabbatical and the vision that started Roblox

A canonical worked example for the May 2026 narrative-style update. Personal-arc shape: instinct → detour → rejection → philosophical pivot → vision moment → snap → company starts → reflection.

## Post body (final shipped version)

```
David Baszucki says he almost spent two years applying for CEO jobs before founding Roblox.

By 2002, Baszucki had sold his first company.

He had time, capital, and an instinct he'd carried since he was a kid coding on an Apple II — that someday he wanted to build a world and stay with it for forty years.

Then he ignored that instinct.

"After selling knowledge revolution and taking my 2-year sabbatical, I tried that, you know, that CEO thing when I wasn't using my intuition."

The CEO thing meant applying to run companies he hadn't started. Other people's visions. Other people's worlds.

He didn't get a single offer.

"What a mistake, right?"

A founder isn't a CEO. A founder is a world-builder.

Then one night, a vision arrived.

"I had a bit of a — it's almost like a vision where I was saying, whoa, you can't be logical on this. You have to be intuitive."

He saw himself on a logical path that ran into a wall. To the side was another path. Worldbuilder. Creators. A platform.

"I was on this path and there was like this big barrier there, but this path which was more worldbuilder creators is like boom, I got it."

That night, Roblox started.

Two decades later, Roblox is a $50 billion platform.

The two-year detour almost canceled all of it.

Most founders never come back from a detour like that. They keep applying to companies they didn't start. They build someone else's clock.

How many people are walking past their own vision because the logical path looks safer?
```

## Why this works

- **Hook (14 words):** named subject (Baszucki) + specific number (two years) + dramatic reversal ("applying for CEO jobs") + named platform (Roblox). Verb is `says`. No recency words.
- **Sentence-length diversity:** the long Apple II / forty-year-instinct sentence (33 words — the one allowed exception) does the narrative-setup work that "Athens 500 BC" does in the Andreessen post. Shorter punches around it ("Then he ignored that instinct." / "He didn't get a single offer.") create the rhythm.
- **Real arc, not insight stack:** instinct → detour → rejection → philosophical pivot → vision moment → snap → company starts → cascade. Each beat earns the next instead of jumping between claims.
- **Parallel triplet for the vision visual:** "Worldbuilder. Creators. A platform." mirrors "No advertising. No commerce. No spam." in the Andreessen post — three short fragments deployed deliberately as a beat, not as filler.
- **Engagement Q tied to actual tension:** "How many people are walking past their own vision because the logical path looks safer?" — anyone reading can answer with their own situation. Not a button-press binary.
- **PAS P.S.:** personalized para 1 opens the wound (logical years are the most expensive); para 2 is the fixed playbook plug.

## What the agent should learn from this

1. **Open the post with story setup, not a claim.** "By 2002, Baszucki had sold his first company." establishes the world before the conflict.
2. **Use the one allowed >15-word sentence on the heaviest beat.** The Apple II / forty-year instinct sentence carries the character setup — it earns the length.
3. **Vary sentence length deliberately.** Short fragment → narrative beat → quote → fragment. Don't get stuck in any one rhythm.
4. **Parallel triplets are powerful when sparingly deployed.** "Worldbuilder. Creators. A platform." is one beat — not the post's default rhythm.
5. **End the body on a cascade + reflection that earns the engagement Q.** The contrast between "Roblox is a $50 billion platform" and "the two-year detour almost canceled all of it" is what gives the open Q its punch.
6. **The engagement Q should pull the reader into the story's tension applied to their own life.** Not a button-press — an invitation.
